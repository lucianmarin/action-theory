## 2. The Geometry of Action Propagation

To prove the equivalence, we must evaluate the action $S$ of a stable pattern as it moves through the emergent causal network.

### 2.1 The Internal Action Rate (Proper Time)
For a stable action pattern $[h]$, its internal stability requires a constant "tick rate" of fundamental action compositions. This intrinsic, internal action-count is the emergent **proper time** $\tau$. 
Because the pattern is stable and the underlying action network is isotropic (yielding emergent Lorentz symmetry), the total effective action $S$ of the history must be proportional to the invariant internal action-count:
$$ S = - \mu \int d\tau $$
where $\mu$ is the invariant internal action density (the topological stiffness of the pattern).

### 2.2 The Emergent Metric and the Roadblock Limit
In the emergent macroscopic limit, the causal ordering $\alpha \prec \beta$ yields the invariant interval:
$$ d\tau = dt \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} $$
where $v$ is the propagation rate of the pattern through the emergent spatial directions, and $c$ is the null boundary (the Roadblock).

Substituting this into the action integral gives the effective Lagrangian $L$:
$$ L = - \mu c^2 \sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}} $$

### 2.3 Deriving the Energy Generator
Energy $E$ is the Hamiltonian—the generator of emergent time translations. Using the standard Legendre transform $E = \frac{\partial L}{\partial v}v - L$, we obtain:
$$ E = \frac{\mu c^2}{\sqrt{1 - \frac{v^2}{c^2}}} $$
