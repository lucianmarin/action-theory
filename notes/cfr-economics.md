# CFR Economics — Action as the Unit of Value

**Layer III.** Status: *heuristic translation; converges with praxeology.*

> **Thesis:** An economy is a network of admissible action compositions.
> Value is expansion of an agent's admissible futures; capital is stored
> freedom.

---

## 0. Scope and honesty

This is **not** derived from `(𝔄, ∘)`. It is a lens that reframes economics
using CFR vocabulary. It converges independently with the praxeological
tradition, which already takes *human action* as its primitive.

**Category-error warning:** economic "actions" (trades, production acts) are
emergent, macroscopic, and intentional — unlike the pre-geometric actions of
Layer I. We borrow the logic, not the physics.

---

## 1. Economics already starts from action

Mises' *Human Action* grounds economics in the "action axiom": humans act
purposefully to move from a less- to a more-satisfied state. CFR supplies an
ontological substrate for exactly this intuition:

```
economy = network of admissible action compositions
```

Where praxeology posits action as an axiom, CFR proposes action as the
literal furniture of reality — so economics, on this view, studies a
high-level expression of the same primitive.

## 3. Production, value, capital

- **Production** = reconfiguring stable action patterns. Matter and energy
  are themselves stable action patterns (§ physics layer), so production is
  action-pattern → action-pattern transformation.
- **Value** is subjective and *forward-looking*: an agent values what expands
  its perceived `N(h)`. This reframes marginal utility as marginal
  freedom-expansion.
- **Capital** is *stored freedom*: a tool, a patent, a school, or a legal
  system is valuable because it raises the dimension of futures available to
  its users.

---

## 4. Markets as freedom geometry

- **Prices** are signals about scarcity and constraint — compressed
  information about `C`.
- **The market process** (Hayek) is a distributed search over `N(h)`: no
  central planner can enumerate the option space, so decentralized discovery
  is required.
- **Competition** keeps `dim N(h)` high by preventing any single agent from
  collapsing the option space (monopoly = local Roadblock on competitors'
  futures).

---

## 5. Entrepreneurship and innovation

- The **entrepreneur** (Schumpeter) explores regions of `N(h)` others have not
  mapped.
- **Creative destruction** is a local Roadblock for incumbents (their
  `N(h)` collapses) coupled with a global *increase* in the economy's freedom
  geometry. CFR makes this trade-off explicit: policy must weigh local
  Roadblocks against global `↑ V_F`.

---

## 6. Business cycle and crisis

- **Boom:** expanding `V_F`, rising rank of the economic freedom geometry.
- **Recession:** contraction of `V_F`; option space narrows.
- **Systemic crisis:** a **Roadblock cascade** — one channel's collapse
  removes admissible continuations from neighboring channels
  (`N(h_i)=∅ ⇒ N(h_j)` shrinks). Financial contagion and supply-chain
  collapse are textbook cascades.
- **Policy aim:** prevent cascades by preserving redundant continuations.

---

## 7. Efficiency vs. resilience

Hyper-efficiency shrinks the option space to a single "optimal" path —
driving `V_F` toward zero and the system toward a Roadblock.

- **Just-in-time** logistics = high efficiency, low `V_F`, high fragility.
- **Redundancy, diversity, slack** = lower short-run yield, high `V_F`,
  high resilience.

> **CFR economics optimizes the dimension of admissible futures, not merely
> short-run yield.**

---

## 8. Institutions: constraints that enable

A constraint `C` can either enable or suppress freedom:

| Constraint type | Effect on `dim N(h)` | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Enabling | raises effective freedom | rule of law, property rights, contracts, standards |
| Suppressing | lowers freedom | red tape, monopoly, corruption, arbitrary expropriation |

Good institutions are *freedom-generating constraints*: they make more
coordinated futures admissible (trust, exchange, long-horizon investment).

---

## 9. Development as freedom

This is the economic twin of Sen's capability approach:

- **Poverty** = a binding constraint on `N(h)`.
- **Development** = expansion of the economic freedom geometry for the poor.
- **Metrics:** beyond GDP, track *option-space expansion* — access to
  education, credit, health, mobility, and legal recourse as dimensions of
  `N(h)`.

---

## 10. Relationship to game theory

- **Game theory** = effective theory of strategic agents with payoffs,
  beliefs, and information. Use it for *incentives*.
- **CFR** = topology of the *option space* in which the game is played.
  Use it for *resilience and possibility*.

They are complementary: game theory tells you how players behave; CFR tells
you how much room they have to behave in.

---

## 11. CFR-inspired indicators (speculative)

*Tag: speculative, not derived.*

- **Option-Space Index (OSI):** estimated `dim N(h)` for a representative
  agent/firm (access to markets, finance, skills, legal recourse).
- **Roadblock-Risk Index (RRI):** probability of a Roadblock cascade in
  critical networks (finance, energy, supply chains).
- **Interference Index (II):** degree to which policies/subsystems cancel
  each other's effects.

---

## 12. Limits

- Heuristic, not derived from Layer I.
- Human intention, meaning, and power are not captured by `N(h)` alone;
  they require game theory, political economy, and sociology.
- Risk of misuse: "maximize freedom" can be invoked to justify harmful
  deregulation. Guardrails in `cfr-society.md`.
